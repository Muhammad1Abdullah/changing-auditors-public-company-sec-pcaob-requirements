# Changing Auditors for a Public Company: SEC Requirements, Form 8-K & How to Hire a New PCAOB Audit Firm

Changing auditors for a public company is more than a routine vendor decision. It is a regulated transition that can affect SEC reporting, audit committee oversight, financial statement audits, and investor disclosures.

Whether your current auditor has resigned, your audit committee is evaluating another firm, or your company needs a new PCAOB-registered auditor before its next filing, understanding the process is essential.

**A U.S. public company changing its independent auditor generally must evaluate SEC Form 8-K Item 4.01 disclosure requirements, obtain appropriate audit committee authorization, and ensure that its successor auditor meets applicable independence and PCAOB requirements.**

For domestic registrants subject to Form 8-K, a reportable change in certifying accountant generally must be reported within four business days. The company may also need to disclose disagreements and reportable events involving its previous auditor.

The new auditor must perform appropriate engagement acceptance procedures, including the predecessor-auditor communications required by PCAOB standards.

This guide explains the rules, common transition risks, filing requirements, costs, and practical steps involved in hiring a new public company audit firm.

## Quick Answer: How Does a Public Company Change Auditors?

A public company typically follows these steps when replacing its independent audit firm:

1. Establish the business reason for considering a change.
2. Obtain the necessary audit committee approvals.
3. Evaluate the proposed successor firm's PCAOB registration, independence, qualifications, and capacity.
4. Coordinate the appropriate termination or resignation process with the existing auditor.
5. Determine whether Form 8-K Item 4.01 disclosure is required.
6. Arrange the predecessor-auditor communications required under PCAOB AS 2610.
7. Complete engagement acceptance and appoint the successor auditor.
8. Transfer relevant audit information through authorized procedures.
9. Address opening balances, accounting matters, and audit planning.
10. Monitor reporting deadlines and complete the transition.

The actual sequence can vary.

For example, a company may evaluate several firms and begin preliminary discussions before its existing auditor resigns.

However, a successor auditor cannot simply accept an engagement without completing the required acceptance procedures.

A properly managed change should protect audit quality, maintain reporting continuity, and reduce avoidable delays.

## When Should a Public Company Consider Changing Auditors?

Not every audit-related frustration justifies replacing a firm.

However, several circumstances may lead management or the audit committee to evaluate alternatives.

### 1. The Existing Auditor Resigns

An audit firm may resign because of business, staffing, independence, risk, or engagement-related considerations.

The company must evaluate the applicable SEC disclosure requirements and determine how its upcoming reporting obligations will be met.

### 2. The Audit Committee Dismisses the Current Firm

The audit committee may determine that a different auditor would better serve the company's needs.

Potential considerations include industry expertise, public-company audit experience, staffing, engagement structure, or audit quality.

The decision should be supported by appropriate governance and documentation.

### 3. The Auditor Declines Reappointment

A firm may complete its current engagement but decline to serve in the next period.

The company should begin successor-auditor planning early enough to avoid unnecessary filing pressure.

### 4. The Company Needs Different Industry Expertise

A company entering a new industry, completing a significant acquisition, or expanding internationally may require additional accounting and auditing capabilities.

The audit committee may evaluate whether its existing firm has the appropriate resources and experience.

### 5. The Company Is Preparing for Significant Transactions

IPO preparation, reverse mergers, business combinations, and major financing transactions can change audit requirements.

An issuer may need an independent registered public accounting firm with relevant capital-markets experience.

### 6. Audit Costs or Engagement Structure Are Being Reassessed

Audit committees may review whether the existing engagement's fees, staffing, timetable, and resources remain appropriate.

Lower cost alone should not determine the selection.

The proposed firm must be capable of completing the required audit procedures under applicable standards.

### 7. Independence or Regulatory Concerns Arise

Auditor independence issues may prevent the existing firm from continuing an engagement.

These situations require careful evaluation because independence requirements cannot simply be waived to accommodate a preferred transition timeline.

## SEC Requirements for Changing Auditors

The SEC has specific disclosure requirements for changes in an issuer's certifying accountant.

For domestic issuers, the primary provisions are:

- Form 8-K Item 4.01.
- Regulation S-K Item 304.
- Applicable audit committee requirements.
- Related SEC financial reporting rules.

The precise obligations depend on the registrant, the relevant event, and its reporting status.

Foreign private issuers and certain other registrants may be subject to different reporting forms and requirements.

The [SEC reporting guidance](https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-corporation-finance/financial-reporting-manual/frm-topic-4) provides additional discussion of changes in accountants.

### What Triggers Form 8-K Item 4.01?

Item 4.01 generally addresses two categories of events.

**Departure of an independent accountant:** An auditor resigns, declines reappointment, or is dismissed.

**Engagement of a new independent accountant:** The company appoints a new firm to audit its financial statements.

Both events can trigger disclosure obligations.

A company should not assume that reporting the former auditor's departure automatically satisfies all disclosures required when a successor is engaged.

If the events occur on different dates, the reporting analysis may differ for each event.

### Form 8-K Auditor Change Deadline

A reportable change under Item 4.01 is generally subject to a **four-business-day filing deadline**.

This is a regulatory deadline, not a recommended planning period.

For illustration, consider a domestic registrant whose independent auditor resigns on Monday, November 9, 2026.

Assuming the usual business-day calculation, no intervening holidays, and no special circumstances, the four-business-day deadline would be Friday, November 13, 2026.

Actual deadline calculations must account for applicable SEC rules, federal holidays, and the specific triggering event.

The company should coordinate promptly with SEC reporting professionals and legal counsel.

### Information Commonly Required in the Disclosure

Depending on the circumstances, the company may need to disclose:

- Whether the auditor resigned, declined reappointment, or was dismissed.
- The applicable date.
- Whether the change was recommended or approved by the board or audit committee.
- Whether prior audit reports included specified modifications.
- Relevant disagreements with the former auditor.
- Reportable events under Regulation S-K Item 304.
- Information about a newly engaged auditor.
- Certain consultations with the successor auditor before engagement.

The precise disclosure requirements are defined by Regulation S-K Item 304.

For the controlling legal provisions, consult [Regulation S-K Item 304](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-229/section-229.304).

## Form 8-K Item 4.01: Disagreements and Reportable Events

One of the most important aspects of changing auditors for a public company is determining whether disagreements or reportable events must be disclosed.

These terms have specific regulatory meanings.

They should not be interpreted solely according to ordinary business usage.

### What Counts as an Auditor Disagreement?

Under Regulation S-K Item 304, a reportable disagreement can involve matters such as:

- Accounting principles or practices.
- Financial statement disclosures.
- Auditing scope.
- Auditing procedures.

A disagreement may be reportable even if it was ultimately resolved to the former auditor's satisfaction.

The analysis depends on the regulatory definition, the facts, and the applicable reporting period.

Not every preliminary discussion or routine accounting question qualifies.

However, management should not assume that a disagreement is irrelevant simply because it was resolved before the auditor changed.

### What Are Reportable Events?

Regulation S-K Item 304 also identifies certain circumstances involving accounting and audit matters that may require disclosure.

Depending on the facts, these can include specified internal-control issues, concerns about management representations, and other matters described in the regulation.

Because the definitions are technical, companies should evaluate them with securities counsel and qualified accounting professionals.

### Why These Disclosures Matter

Investors may reasonably want to understand whether an auditor change was routine or involved significant accounting concerns.

Incomplete disclosure can create regulatory and credibility risks.

The company should gather relevant communications and supporting documentation before preparing the filing.

## What Is an Exhibit 16 Auditor Letter?

A former auditor may be required to provide a letter addressing the company's disclosures concerning the change.

This is commonly referred to as an Exhibit 16 letter.

Under Regulation S-K Item 304(a)(3), the company provides the former accountant with the relevant disclosure no later than the filing date and requests a letter stating whether the auditor agrees with the company's statements.

If the former auditor disagrees, the letter identifies the respects in which it disagrees.

### What if the Letter Is Not Available Immediately?

If the letter is unavailable at the time of the initial filing, Regulation S-K provides a mechanism for later submission.

Generally, the registrant requests the letter promptly, with a ten-business-day outside period following the relevant filing, and files it by amendment within two business days of receipt.

Specific requirements and circumstances should be verified against the current regulation.

The company should not treat the Exhibit 16 process as an optional administrative detail.

### What if the Former Auditor Refuses?

The SEC's Financial Reporting Manual addresses situations in which a former accountant refuses to furnish the requested letter.

Companies should evaluate the applicable disclosure and amendment requirements rather than silently omitting the issue.

The [SEC change guidance](https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-corporation-finance/financial-reporting-manual/frm-topic-4) discusses this situation.

## Audit Committee Responsibilities When Replacing an Auditor

For listed companies subject to the applicable requirements, the audit committee is directly responsible for the appointment, compensation, retention, and oversight of the independent registered public accounting firm.

The auditor reports directly to the audit committee.

These responsibilities are central to auditor selection.

Management can support the process by gathering proposals, coordinating meetings, and preparing financial information.

However, management support does not replace the audit committee's responsibilities.

### Questions the Audit Committee Should Address

Before recommending or approving a change, consider:

- Why is a new auditor being considered?
- What problems should the transition solve?
- What qualifications are essential?
- Can the proposed firm meet the company's reporting timetable?
- Is the firm independent?
- Does it have relevant public-company experience?
- How will audit quality be evaluated?
- What transition risks require attention?
- Are the proposed fees consistent with the expected scope?
- What documentation will support the decision?

The SEC's [audit committee requirements](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2003/04/standards-relating-listed-company-audit-committees) explain the underlying oversight responsibilities.

## How to Hire a New PCAOB-Registered Audit Firm

For a public company, hiring a new auditor requires more diligence than comparing proposals.

The audit committee needs a firm capable of performing the engagement under the applicable professional standards while maintaining independence.

### Step 1: Verify PCAOB Registration

Start by confirming that the proposed firm is appropriately registered with the PCAOB.

The PCAOB maintains a searchable database of registered public accounting firms.

Use the official [PCAOB firm directory](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/registration/registered-firms) to review registration information.

Registration is an essential threshold consideration for relevant issuer audits.

However, registration alone is not a guarantee of engagement quality, appropriate experience, or eligibility for every proposed engagement.

### Step 2: Review SEC Audit Experience

Ask whether the firm has experience with:

- SEC registrants.
- Annual Form 10-K audits.
- Quarterly financial statement reviews.
- Public-company accounting issues.
- Audit committee communications.
- Significant estimates and disclosures.
- Internal controls and applicable SOX requirements.

Experience with private-company audits does not automatically establish readiness for an issuer engagement.

### Step 3: Evaluate Industry Knowledge

Industry-specific transactions can materially affect audit planning.

A public technology company, investment-related business, manufacturer, and multinational issuer may face different reporting challenges.

Discuss the firm's experience with similar accounting issues and operational environments.

### Step 4: Assess Independence

The successor firm must evaluate independence under applicable SEC and PCAOB requirements.

Potential issues may include financial relationships, prohibited services, business relationships, or other conflicts.

The company should disclose relevant relationships during the evaluation.

Independence must be assessed before engagement acceptance and maintained throughout the relevant period.

### Step 5: Review Engagement Staffing

Ask who will serve as the engagement partner and which team members will be responsible for the work.

Consider:

- Partner involvement.
- Experience with SEC filings.
- Availability during reporting periods.
- Technical consultation resources.
- Quality review processes.
- Coordination with international teams, where relevant.

### Step 6: Evaluate the Audit Plan

A credible proposal should explain the proposed engagement scope, planning approach, major accounting issues, deliverables, and anticipated timeline.

The firm should also identify material uncertainties that prevent a reliable commitment.

### Step 7: Compare Audit Fees

Request a written proposal describing:

- Initial engagement scope.
- Expected audit periods.
- Quarterly review requirements.
- Significant subsidiaries.
- Potential additional procedures.
- Out-of-pocket expenses.
- Circumstances that may change the fee.

Selecting the lowest bidder without evaluating the proposed scope creates unnecessary risk.

For additional guidance, review [PCAOB audit costs](https://www.mbpgloballlp.com/insight-and-news/how-much-does-a-pcaob-audit-cost).

### Step 8: Complete Acceptance Procedures

The successor auditor must complete the appropriate engagement acceptance process before final acceptance.

This includes required predecessor-auditor communications under PCAOB AS 2610.

The firm may make a proposal before these communications are completed, but final acceptance is subject to the standard's requirements.

## PCAOB AS 2610: Predecessor and Successor Auditor Communications

PCAOB AS 2610 addresses the communications required when a successor auditor considers accepting an engagement previously handled by another auditor.

These communications serve an important purpose.

The predecessor may have information about management integrity, accounting disagreements, audit matters, or other issues affecting the successor's acceptance decision.

### What Must the Successor Auditor Ask?

AS 2610 identifies inquiries concerning matters such as:

- Management integrity.
- Disagreements over accounting principles or audit procedures.
- Communications regarding fraud and illegal acts.
- Certain internal-control matters.
- Reasons for changing auditors.
- Related-party relationships and significant unusual transactions.

The prospective client generally authorizes the predecessor auditor to respond.

If permission is refused or restricted, the successor must consider the implications.

### Can the New Auditor Access Previous Audit Workpapers?

The successor auditor should request authorization to review relevant predecessor workpapers.

However, access is not automatic.

The predecessor determines the extent of access and what may be copied, subject to applicable professional obligations and arrangements.

The successor remains responsible for obtaining sufficient appropriate audit evidence for its own opinion.

Reviewing prior workpapers does not transfer responsibility for the previous auditor's conclusions.

Read the official [PCAOB AS 2610 standard](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2610) for detailed requirements.

## How Long Does It Take to Change Auditors?

There is no universal timeline for replacing a public company auditor.

The transition may involve several stages that occur over weeks or months, depending on the engagement.

Factors influencing timing include:

- Company size and complexity.
- Number of subsidiaries.
- International operations.
- Reporting deadlines.
- Predecessor-auditor communication.
- Financial statement readiness.
- Internal-control issues.
- Significant accounting estimates.
- Auditor capacity.
- Open regulatory matters.

Companies should distinguish the regulatory reporting deadline from engagement acceptance, audit planning, and audit execution.

## Auditor Change vs. PCAOB Re-Audit

Changing auditors does not automatically mean a company must re-audit previously audited financial statements.

These are distinct events.

An auditor change generally involves appointing a successor for current or future audit work.

A re-audit involves a new auditor performing audit procedures and issuing an opinion on financial statements that were previously audited.

A re-audit may become necessary in certain transaction or reporting circumstances, but the requirement must be evaluated based on the specific facts.

### When a Re-Audit May Become Relevant

Examples requiring further analysis include:

- Certain capital-markets transactions.
- Financial statement reporting issues.
- Circumstances affecting reliance on prior audit reports.
- Regulatory or registration statement requirements.
- Engagements specifically involving historical financial statements.

The successor auditor must obtain sufficient appropriate evidence for any opinion it issues.

A review of predecessor workpapers alone is not sufficient to support a re-audit opinion.

## Can a Public Company Change Auditors During an SEC Filing Period?

A public company may experience an auditor change close to a financial reporting deadline.

However, the timing can introduce operational risks.

The company needs to evaluate whether the successor can complete the necessary procedures in time to support applicable filings.

Potential concerns include:

- Incomplete workpaper availability.
- Unresolved accounting estimates.
- Missing supporting records.
- Significant internal-control findings.
- Required audit committee communications.
- Unfinished engagement acceptance procedures.

An auditor change does not automatically extend a company's SEC reporting deadlines.

Management should coordinate the transition with legal counsel, the audit committee, and the relevant audit professionals.

## Common Mistakes When Changing SEC Auditors

### Choosing a Firm Based Only on Price

An attractive quote is not enough if the proposed firm lacks the required capacity or qualifications.

### Waiting Until the Reporting Deadline

Late auditor selection can create unnecessary pressure.

### Failing to Evaluate Independence

Independence issues may prevent a proposed firm from accepting the engagement.

### Ignoring Form 8-K Requirements

A reportable auditor change may require prompt disclosure even when the company expects to appoint a successor soon.

### Assuming Disagreements Are Unimportant

Resolved disagreements may still be reportable under the applicable definitions.

### Restricting Predecessor Communications

Limitations on access to the former auditor can affect successor engagement acceptance.

### Confusing an Auditor Change With a Re-Audit

A replacement engagement does not automatically require historical re-audit work.

### Underestimating Transition Documentation

Incomplete records can complicate planning and affect the ability to obtain audit evidence.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a public company change auditors?

Yes. A public company can change its independent auditor, subject to applicable governance, SEC disclosure, independence, and professional requirements.

### Does changing auditors require Form 8-K?

For domestic SEC registrants, reportable changes in certifying accountants generally trigger Form 8-K Item 4.01 requirements.

The specific disclosures depend on the facts and the relevant event.

### How quickly must a company report an auditor change?

A reportable Item 4.01 event generally requires a Form 8-K filing within four business days.

### Who approves the appointment of a new public company auditor?

For listed issuers subject to the applicable audit committee requirements, the audit committee is directly responsible for appointment, compensation, retention, and oversight of the independent auditor.

### Does the new auditor need PCAOB registration?

A firm performing an issuer audit that requires PCAOB registration must be appropriately registered. Registration can be checked through the PCAOB's official directory.

### What is a successor auditor?

A successor auditor is an auditor considering or accepting an engagement following another auditor.

PCAOB AS 2610 addresses relevant predecessor-successor communications.

### Must the successor contact the previous auditor?

PCAOB AS 2610 requires specified communications with the predecessor auditor as part of engagement acceptance, subject to its provisions.

### What is an Exhibit 16 letter?

It is a letter from the former accountant addressing whether the accountant agrees with the company's disclosures about the auditor change under Regulation S-K Item 304.

### What if the previous auditor disagrees with management?

The company must evaluate whether the circumstances constitute a reportable disagreement or reportable event under Regulation S-K Item 304.

Some resolved disagreements may still require disclosure.

### Can a company change auditors immediately before filing Form 10-K?

A change may occur near a filing deadline, but the company must assess whether the successor can complete required engagement acceptance and audit procedures in time.

The change does not automatically extend the filing deadline.

### Does changing auditors require a new audit of previous years?

Not automatically. A re-audit is a separate engagement, and whether it is necessary depends on the company's reporting circumstances.

### How much does it cost to hire a new PCAOB audit firm?

Costs depend on the audit scope, company complexity, reporting periods, transactions, locations, and other engagement requirements. A qualified firm should provide a scope-based proposal rather than a universal flat fee.

### Can a company hire a smaller PCAOB-registered audit firm?

Potentially, yes, provided the firm meets applicable registration, independence, qualification, and engagement requirements.

The audit committee should evaluate the firm's capability and resources for the company's specific needs.

### How do I find a PCAOB-registered auditor?

Start with the PCAOB's public firm directory, then evaluate relevant issuer-audit experience, independence, staff capacity, engagement leadership, and reporting timelines.

### Can an international company use a U.S. PCAOB audit firm?

Depending on the reporting entity, its regulatory obligations, the firm's eligibility, and the scope of the engagement, a U.S. PCAOB-registered firm may be appropriate.

Foreign private issuers may have different SEC disclosure requirements when changing accountants.

### What should a CFO prepare before contacting a new audit firm?

Prepare recent SEC filings, financial statements, information about the current auditor, reporting deadlines, significant accounting matters, subsidiaries, and the proposed audit scope.
